      Contract controls

      The strike is the locked-in price the option lets you trade at. It is set to roughly today’s price — change it freely.

      A call is the right to buy at the strike (worth more if the stock rises); a put the right to sell (worth more if it falls). Long means you buy that option and pay the premium. Short means you write it — you collect the premium up front and take on the obligation if the buyer exercises.

      How to read these numbers

      • Fair price is what the maths says the option is worth today, judged three different ways from how this stock has actually moved over the last two years. If the market quotes much more, the option is expensive; much less, cheap — by these models’ lights.
      • The stock outlook is not a promise. It is the middle of thousands of simulated futures built from the same two years — the range around it is where 9 out of 10 of those simulations landed.
      • Break-even is the strike shifted by the premium: a buyer only comes out ahead at expiry once the stock has moved far enough to repay what the option cost. Before expiry, an in-the-money option is usually worth more sold than exercised, because exercising collects only the intrinsic part and throws away the remaining time value.
      • Writing an option is not the mirror of buying one. A buyer can lose at most the premium paid; a writer’s profit is capped at the premium collected while the loss can be far larger — unlimited for a written call. Brokers demand margin for a reason.
      • Everything here assumes the future rhymes with the recent past. Real markets are under no such obligation.

      Volatility regimes found by Baum-Welch

      Fitted volatility regimes

      Baum-Welch prices nothing by itself. It reads the return history and reports which volatility regimes generated it, how long each one lasts and which is running now — the volatility the two pricing models then use. Drift is estimated from the same short history and is far noisier than volatility; treat those figures with more suspicion.

      Transition matrix

      Regime transition probabilities

      Each row is today’s regime, each column tomorrow’s. The diagonal is how likely the market is to stay where it is.

      How this is calculated

      Employee options are not tradable and holders exercise early for reasons no model sees, so ASC 718 values them over the expected term rather than the contractual one, then reduces the expense by the options expected to be forfeited before they vest.
