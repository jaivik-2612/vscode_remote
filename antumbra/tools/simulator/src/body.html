Notes

What happens behind the screen is explained here as you go. Notes never cover the phone's controls: on the phone they appear only on the bare home screen or in the Welcome screen's title strip. One warning comes as a sheet you close instead: the first time you choose Create, that the simulated Persistent Storage is not encrypted.

    About

    An interactive model of Antumbra, the Tails-based privacy OS for the OnePlus 7T Pro (Debian 13, Phosh, mainline Linux). Its screens, labels, defaults, messages and rules come from the project's repository and from screenshots of the real image running in a virtual machine (QEMU).

    It is not the operating system. Nothing here runs Antumbra's code, and Tor, Android and the camera are acted out. The page makes no network request at all: even its fonts are embedded. Antumbra is an arm64 image of about 1.3 GB. An iPhone cannot run another operating system: iOS allows apps and web pages no virtual machine (no hypervisor, no JIT). Even a server, emulating the phone's processor, needs minutes to reach the Welcome screen.

    The project itself is alpha and has not yet booted on a physical phone.

    Live state

    The real OS shows no Tor indicator or notification. To see the phase there: switch on Administration at the Welcome screen, then run sudo antumbra-tor-connect status in Console.

    What to try
    1. At the Welcome screen choose Create (erases the data partition), type a passphrase of 12 characters or more twice, switch on Android apps (experimental), then press Start.
    2. Choose Through bridges and type a snowflake line to see the real refusal, then an obfs4 192.0.2.1:443 … line.
    3. In the session: pull down the top bar (or tap it) for quick settings and join a Wi-Fi network; Tor then connects (acted out past 14 %).
    4. Swipe up from the bottom (or tap the pill) for the apps. Open Camera: allow it and watch the pop-up camera rise.
    5. Open the Android folder once Android has started; open F-Droid.
    6. Write a note in Text Editor and save it in Persistent, or one in Documents.
    7. Restart from the power menu (top-right button in quick settings). Unlock with a wrong passphrase, then the right one: only the note in Persistent is still there.
    8. In Console, try ip a, whoami, sudo antumbra-tor-connect status, cat /run/antumbra/selfcheck.status, torify curl example.org, help.
    Known issues (from the project's docs)
    • The pop-up camera has no drop protection: do not walk with it raised or push it down. It shows only that the front camera is in use; rear cameras have no indicator.
    • The camera prompt does not protect the cameras: any program in the session, Tor Browser included, can use them without any prompt (directly, through PipeWire, or by approving itself in the portal), whatever you answer.
    • Android is a weaker sandbox (privileged container, no SELinux), TCP only through Tor: no UDP, VPN, IPv6 or .onion; no camera; security patches frozen at 2026-09-27.
    • No Tor Connection assistant: bridges are typed again at every boot. Snowflake and IPv6 bridges are refused. No bridge has connected through Antumbra yet.
    • Tor Browser for arm64 is an alpha release with no AppArmor profile; there is no Unsafe Browser.
    • A screen lock without a passphrase does not protect anything. The Welcome screen does not read back the settings stored in Persistent Storage.

    Real and simulated

    Taken from the real system:

    • Every Welcome screen label, option, default and error message, and which fields are enabled when (GTK's "sensitivity"). The bridge check is a direct copy of the OS's own checking code (bridge_lines, normalise_bridges).
    • The order in which Start applies the settings, and its failure texts; the Persistent Storage features; the unlock flow.
    • Tor's own phase names; the VM's log lines up to 14 % (the lines past 14 %, and every bridge line, never appeared in a VM log); the three connection modes.
    • The theme: palette, the image's own Roboto font, radii, the gesture bar at the bottom (108×4 px), the three wallpapers, the Material Symbols and Adwaita icons, the app grid and dock.
    • Quick-settings tiles and their states, the lock screen's texts, the end-session dialog, the camera prompt, the Android launcher's notifications, F-Droid's permission prompt.

    Acted out or approximated:

    • Timings (see above), Tor's progress past 14 %, Wi-Fi networks (invented names, any password of 8 characters works), the battery level.
    • The camera shows the VM's colour bars, or one of your own pictures (it stays on the page: nothing is uploaded or stored); there is no live video.
    • Android, F-Droid and Tor Browser are drawn in HTML; Tor Browser shows only a local page of the simulator. Most other apps open to a placeholder.
    • Clock: your device's local time (the real OS runs in UTC until htpdate sets it).
    • Text fields use your device's keyboard; the phone uses squeekboard.
    • The dashed "Simulator" cards are not part of Antumbra. The real OS shows no Tor or F-Droid notification and no drop warning for the camera.
    • The simulated Persistent Storage is NOT encrypted. Its files are kept in plain text in this browser's storage (localStorage) until you use "Erase simulated Persistent Storage". Do not type a passphrase you really use, and do not save real notes. Its passphrase is kept only as a slow salted hash (PBKDF2), and a picture made from your own photo is never stored. Everything else is forgotten at a simulated restart.
